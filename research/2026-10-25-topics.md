## Topic 1 (rank 1): Rates Finally Eased — Then Partly Bounced Back — Three Days Before the Fed's Next Decision

### Why it's trending now
After eight straight weeks of increases, the Mortgage Bankers Association's weekly 30-year fixed average eased to 7.41% for the week ending October 16, 2026, down from 7.50% the week before. ([MPA, "With the Fed's October 28 meeting nearing, mortgage rates ease to 7.41%..."](https://www.mpamag.com/us/mortgage-industry/market-updates/treasury-yields-hold-as-brokers-track-october-fed-outlook/594438)) Daily benchmarks moved even more sharply within the week: Zillow's average 30-year fixed fell to 7.14% on October 17 and held there on October 19, before climbing back to 7.25% on October 20 and 7.23% on October 21. ([NoradaRealEstate, citing Zillow](https://www.noradarealestate.com/?p=86208); [NoradaRealEstate](https://www.noradarealestate.com/?p=86177)) The dip tracked falling Treasury yields amid a flight to safety and growing market expectations that the Fed will hold rates steady at its next meeting. ([MPA](https://www.mpamag.com/us/mortgage-industry/market-updates/treasury-yields-hold-as-brokers-track-october-fed-outlook/594438))

That meeting — the FOMC's October 27–28, 2026 decision — lands three days after this memo. As of October 20, CME FedWatch priced roughly an 83% chance of a hold at the current 3.75%–4.00% target range (set at the September 16 hike), about 17% odds of another quarter-point hike, and effectively no odds of a cut. With inflation running around 3.7%, several Fed officials are reportedly expecting one more hike before year-end — with December, not October, as the more likely point. ([MPA](https://www.mpamag.com/us/mortgage-industry/market-updates/treasury-yields-hold-as-brokers-track-october-fed-outlook/594438)) Meanwhile mortgage applications fell 5.3% in the week ending October 16 even as rates eased — buyers broadly are not rushing in on the dip. ([MPA](https://www.mpamag.com/us/mortgage-industry/market-updates/treasury-yields-hold-as-brokers-track-october-fed-outlook/594438))

This is a live decision point for anyone shopping a rate this week, and it's a different story from the site's recent rate coverage (a hike, then three straight weeks above 7%, then the worst week in four years) — this week it's a partial reversal, right before the next Fed verdict.

### Suggested article
**Title:** "Rates Just Eased for the First Time in 8 Weeks — Then Bounced Back. Here's What the Fed's October 28 Decision Actually Changes for You"

**Angle (Want/Problem/Stakes):**
- *Want:* You want to know whether to lock your rate this week or wait a few more days to see what the Fed does.
- *Problem:* Rates genuinely dropped mid-week — then gave most of it back before the Fed even met. The meeting everyone's watching is three days away, and the most likely outcome (a hold) doesn't actually promise you relief.
- *Stakes:* Treat this week's dip as the start of a trend and wait to lock, and you could be locking at a worse rate once the bounce-back finishes — or worse, right before a December hike some Fed officials are already signaling. Panic-lock at the local high instead, and you give up the few days of real downside that did happen. Either way, the decision you make tonight is based on three days of noise, not a verdict — and you should know that before you act on it.

### Key facts for the article (with citations)
1. MBA's weekly 30-year fixed average eased to 7.41% for the week ending October 16, 2026 — down 9 basis points from 7.50% the prior week, ending an eight-week run of increases. ([MPA](https://www.mpamag.com/us/mortgage-industry/market-updates/treasury-yields-hold-as-brokers-track-october-fed-outlook/594438))
2. Daily rates swung more than the weekly average suggests: Zillow's 30-year fixed fell to 7.14% on Oct 17 and 19, then rose to 7.25% on Oct 20 and 7.23% on Oct 21 — a roughly 11-basis-point round trip within five business days. ([NoradaRealEstate](https://www.noradarealestate.com/?p=86208))

Mortgage rates track the 10-year Treasury yield and the mortgage-backed securities market far more directly than the Fed funds rate itself — a "hold" at the Oct 28 meeting changes none of the inputs that actually moved rates this week, and a cut isn't on the table regardless of outcome. ## Topic 2 (rank 2): A New Credit Score Is Coming to FHA Loans on January 1 — But It Won't Change Anything for You Today

### Why it's trending now
FHA confirmed on October 9, 2026 that VantageScore 4.0 and FICO Score 10T become eligible credit score models for FHA-insured underwriting for case numbers assigned on or after January 1, 2027, alongside an updated "minimum decision credit score" methodology for when a lender submits more than one model. ([ICBA, "FHA updates policy for implementing alternative credit scores," Oct 9, 2026](https://www.icba.org/web/payments/w/fha-updates-policy-for-implementing-alternative-credit-scores); [NAR Washington Report, "FHA sets implementation date for alternative credit score models"](https://www.nar.realtor/news/washington-report/fha-sets-implementation-date-for-alternative-credit-score-models)) This follows FHFA and HUD's joint rollout of VantageScore 4.0 (and FICO 10T) for Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac loans earlier in 2026, which began as an interim phase where approved lenders could choose either Classic FICO or VantageScore 4.0 — not both on one loan. ([VantageScore, "FHFA Director and HUD Secretary Jointly Announce VantageScore 4.0 Implementation for Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac and FHA"](https://vantagescore.com/resources/knowledge-center/fhfa-director-and-hud-secretary-jointly-announce-vantagescore-4-0-implementation-for-fannie-mae-freddie-mac-and-fha))

This is genuinely new ground for RealityCents — no KB article and no prior weekly memo has touched credit score models — and it's a real, dated FHA policy change that affects how buyers qualify. But nearly every source covering it is written for lenders and trade groups, not buyers, and the trade coverage doesn't say plainly that nothing changes for anyone applying between now and January 1, 2027.

### Suggested article
**Title:** "A New Credit Score Is Coming to FHA Loans on January 1, 2027 — Here's What Actually Changes (and What Doesn't)"

**Angle (Want/Problem/Stakes):**
- *Want:* You want to know whether a different credit score will help you qualify, and whether you should buy before or after the switch.
- *Problem:* Headlines about "new credit scores for mortgages" are written for lenders, not buyers — they don't say which score you'll actually be scored under, when, or whether either model is better for someone like you.
- *Stakes:* Assume this helps everyone with thin credit, and you could pass up a purchase today you'd have qualified for anyway — or assume nothing is changing and get blindsided by a different number on a loan with a 2027 case number. Either mistake comes from not knowing what this policy actually does.

### Key facts for the article (with citations)
1. FHA announced on October 9, 2026 that VantageScore 4.0 and FICO Score 10T become eligible alongside Classic FICO for FHA-insured mortgages, for case numbers assigned on or after January 1, 2027. ([ICBA, Oct 9, 2026](https://www.icba.org/web/payments/w/fha-updates-policy-for-implementing-alternative-credit-scores))
2. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac already accept VantageScore 4.0 (and FICO 10T) as of earlier in 2026, under an interim phase: a given lender can choose to deliver loans scored under either Classic FICO or VantageScore 4.0, but a single loan can't mix models. ([VantageScore](https://vantagescore.com/resources/knowledge-center/fhfa-director-and-hud-secretary-jointly-announce-vantagescore-4-0-implementation-for-fannie-mae-freddie-mac-and-fha))
3. Classic FICO is not being retired in this rollout — industry sources report FHA does not plan to sunset it — and until the January 1, 2027 effective date, FHA has told lenders to keep following the existing credit guidance in HUD Handbook 4000.1, meaning nothing changes for a borrower applying today. ([NAR Washington Report](https://www.nar.realtor/news/washington-report/fha-sets-implementation-date-for-alternative-credit-score-models))
4. On a loan where a lender submits more than one score model, all borrowers on that loan must be scored using the same model(s), and if multiple models are submitted to FHA's TOTAL Scorecard, all of them must pass for the loan to receive an "Accept." ([ICBA](https://www.icba.org/web/payments/w/fha-updates-policy-for-implementing-alternative-credit-scores))
5. The practical difference between the models: VantageScore 4.0 and FICO 10T both use "trended" data — up to 24 months of account balance and payment history, not just a snapshot — and VantageScore 4.0 can generate a score for some consumers with too few traditional accounts to be scored under Classic FICO at all. Neither model guarantees a higher score for a given borrower; a buyer should ask their lender which model(s) will actually be pulled rather than assume either direction. (Industry background via VantageScore and HousingWire coverage of the GSE rollout.)
6. Part of the rationale cited by regulators and VantageScore is cost: mortgage-score pricing under VantageScore 4.0 has reportedly dropped to roughly $1 per pull from the credit bureaus, with FHFA citing potential industry-wide savings approaching $1 billion in the first year — a lender-side cost change, not something that by itself changes a buyer's qualifying number. ([VantageScore](https://vantagescore.com/resources/knowledge-center/fhfa-director-announces-immediate-implementation-of-vantagescore-4-0-mortgages-for-fannie-mae-and-freddie-mac))

**Honest nuance to include:** State plainly that this is a "know it's coming" piece, not a "rush to buy before/after" piece — the January 1, 2027 date only governs FHA case numbers assigned after that date, Classic FICO remains valid, and trended/alternative data helps some thin-file borrowers while doing nothing or even scoring others lower. Don't claim this is a guaranteed win for any particular type of buyer.

## Topic 3 (rank 3): Your Future Ohana Unit Could Help You Qualify — But Honolulu's Own Rules Haven't Caught Up Yet

### Why it's trending now
Fannie Mae's Selling Guide Announcement SEL-2025-08 (October 2025) authorized lenders to count rental income from an accessory dwelling unit (ADU) toward a borrower's qualifying income; Desktop Underwriter version 12.1, which implements the rule, went live the weekend of March 21, 2026. ([National Mortgage Professional, "Fannie Mae: ADU Income Now Allowed"](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/fannie-mae-adu-income-now-allowed); [PennyMac Correspondent Announcement 26-29](https://corr.pennymac.com/announcements/announcement-26-29)) Running on a separate but directly relevant track, Hawaii's 2024 Act 39 (SB 3202) requires all four counties — including Honolulu — to amend their zoning ordinances by December 31, 2026 to allow at least two ADUs on residentially zoned lots (counties may still deny a specific permit for genuine utility-capacity reasons). A 2025 bill (HB 516) to repeal that county mandate and replace it with a subsidy/tax-exemption program instead was carried over into the 2026 session rather than passed, and a Honolulu City Council resolution has separately asked the state to repeal Act 39 — so the December 31 deadline currently still stands, but it is actively contested. ([Hawaii Legislature, SB3202 CD1](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/sessions/session2024/bills/SB3202_CD1_.HTM); [Hawaii Legislature, HB516](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/sessions/session2025/bills/HB516_.HTM))

Neither thread is new news this week on its own, but together they're a genuinely unused angle on this site: the mortgage rule and the Hawaii-specific zoning deadline are each real, dated, and relevant to the exact audience RealityCents writes for — buyers trying to use a backyard unit to make an Oahu purchase work — and RealityCents' own house-hacking article only warns about unpermitted units losing their VA appraisal eligibility, without covering either the Fannie Mae conventional income rule or the Act 39 deadline. (Internal: `/knowledge-base/va-loan-house-hacking-hawaii`)

### Suggested article
**Title:** "Your Ohana Unit's Rent Could Now Count Toward Your Mortgage — Here's the Catch Most Buyers Miss"

**Angle (Want/Problem/Stakes):**
- *Want:* You want a backyard ohana or ADU's rental income to help you qualify for an Oahu purchase you otherwise couldn't afford.
- *Problem:* Fannie Mae will let a lender count that income now — but only for a unit that's already legally permitted, and Honolulu's own rules on how many ADUs you're even allowed to build are themselves tied up in a state deadline that a repeal effort is actively trying to undo.
- *Stakes:* Assume an unpermitted ohana's rent will count toward your loan, and you can lose that income mid-underwriting and fail to qualify for the house you offered on. Assume you'll be able to add a second ADU after closing because "the law requires it by December 2026," and you could be planning around a deadline that shifts or disappears before you get there.

### Key facts for the article (with citations)
1. Fannie Mae now allows ADU rental income to count toward a borrower's qualifying income on a purchase or limited cash-out refinance of a one-unit primary residence, effective in Desktop Underwriter as of the weekend of March 21, 2026. ([NMP](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/fannie-mae-adu-income-now-allowed))
2. Limits: only one ADU's income may be used even if multiple ADUs exist on the property, the ADU income used can't exceed 30% of total qualifying income, and the appraisal must be completed on the newer UAD 3.6 form. ([NMP](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/fannie-mae-adu-income-now-allowed))
3. The rule is no longer limited to HomeReady borrowers — any eligible borrower can use it — and Fannie Mae separately expanded ADU eligibility to allow up to three ADUs on one primary dwelling and to cover qualifying manufactured homes. ([NMP](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/fannie-mae-adu-income-now-allowed))
4. Hawaii's 2024 Act 39 requires all four counties to amend their ordinances by December 31, 2026 to allow at least two ADUs on residentially zoned lots statewide, with a narrow exception where a county can show insufficient utility infrastructure. ([Hawaii Legislature, SB3202 CD1](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/sessions/session2024/bills/SB3202_CD1_.HTM))
5. A 2025 bill to repeal that county mandate (HB 516) did not pass and was carried over to the 2026 session instead; separately, a Honolulu City Council resolution has asked the state to repeal Act 39 through the counties' own 2026 legislative package. As of this writing the December 31, 2026 deadline still stands, but readers should not treat it as settled. ([Hawaii Legislature, HB516](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/sessions/session2025/bills/HB516_.HTM))
6. These are two separate thresholds a buyer must check, not one: Fannie Mae's rule only ever applies to a unit that is *already* legally permitted today, regardless of what Act 39 eventually requires counties to allow going forward — a future, not-yet-built ADU can't be counted for income no matter how the zoning law resolves. RealityCents' own house-hacking article already flags that an unpermitted ohana/ADU can't be counted by a lender and can be flagged by a VA appraiser; this rule raises the stakes on getting that permitting question right for conventional buyers specifically. (Internal: `/knowledge-base/va-loan-house-hacking-hawaii`)

**Honest nuance to include:** Be direct that this piece covers two different Hawaii/mortgage storylines that happen to intersect, not one single new development — and that the zoning-law side is unsettled (a live repeal effort exists). Don't tell readers to count on either the income rule or the zoning deadline without verifying their own property's permit status and checking current Honolulu Department of Planning and Permitting rules before writing an offer around either one.
