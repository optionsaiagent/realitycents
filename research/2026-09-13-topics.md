## Topic 1 (Ranked #1): Rates Just Broke Their Own 2026 Record — Again

### Why it's trending now
This is a live, fast-moving story RealityCents has now touched twice: the Aug 3 site article flagged rates hitting a "2026 high" of 6.67%, and last week's research memo (Sept 6) flagged a further move to 6.71% (PMMS) heading into the Sept 15–16 FOMC meeting, framing that meeting as the thing to watch. The new development this week: rates kept climbing *before* the Fed even met. Freddie Mac's PMMS put the 30-year fixed at 6.76% for the week ending September 10 (up from 6.71%), and Optimal Blue's daily benchmark — a faster-moving read most loan officers watch day-to-day — climbed from 6.77% Tuesday to 6.88% Thursday, a fresh 52-week high. The move tracked the 10-year Treasury yield jumping from 4.80% to 4.96% over the week, driven by a hotter-than-expected producer price report and a firm CPI reading — not by anything the Fed did. With the FOMC decision now just two days out (Sept 15–16), this is the clearest real-world proof yet of last week's core lesson: mortgage rates move on inflation data and Treasury yields, not on Fed anticipation alone, and buyers waiting on the Fed for relief are watching the wrong number.

## Topic 2 (Ranked #2): Oahu's Market Just Split in Two — Houses Near a Record, Condos Getting Cheaper

### Why it's trending now
Honolulu Board of Realtors data for August 2026, reported September 7, shows single-family and condo markets moving in opposite directions in the same month: single-family median price jumped 12.2% year-over-year to $1,240,000 (second-highest month ever, behind June 2026's $1,242,500), while the condo median fell 1% to $510,000 with sales down 6.6%. HBR's own explanation ties the gap directly to carrying costs — insurance, HOA fees, and reserve funding — which is the sharpest real-world data point yet on how the ongoing condo insurance crisis is now visibly reshaping prices, not just headlines.

Sources:
- [Oahu condominium prices fell in August; home costs surged — Honolulu Star-Advertiser](https://www.staradvertiser.com/2026/09/07/hawaii-news/oahu-condominium-prices-fell-in-august-home-costs-surged/) (Sept 7, 2026)
- Mirror: [Hawaii News Network](https://www.hawaiinewsnetwork.com/oahu-condominium-prices-fell-in-august-home-costs-surged/) (Sept 7, 2026)
- Honolulu Board of Realtors monthly statistics: [hicentral.com](https://www.hicentral.com/)

### Suggested article
**Title:** "Houses Near a Record High, Condos Getting Cheaper: What Oahu's Split Market Means for Your Purchase"

**Want / Problem / Stakes:**
- *Want:* You want a straight answer on whether now is a smart time to buy on Oahu — house or condo.
- *Problem:* The market isn't telling one story right now, it's telling two contradictory ones at once, and generic "Hawaii housing" headlines flatten them into noise that doesn't help you decide.
- *Stakes:* Assume houses are "too expensive right now" and you miss the window before prices climb further — or grab a condo because it looks like a discount without pricing in a five-figure special assessment, and the savings evaporate inside two years.

### Key facts to include (with citations)
1. Oahu's single-family median price hit $1,240,000 in August 2026, up 12.2% year-over-year — the second-highest month on record, behind only June 2026's $1,242,500 (Honolulu Star-Advertiser, citing HBR/HiCentral data, Sept 7, 2026).
2. Single-family sales rose 1.9% year-over-year to 264 closings, from 259 a year earlier (Honolulu Star-Advertiser, Sept 7, 2026).
3. The condo median price fell 1% year-over-year to $510,000 (from $515,000), while condo sales dropped 6.6% to 365 closings (Honolulu Star-Advertiser, Sept 7, 2026).
4. Condo inventory rose roughly 4.1% year-over-year, giving buyers more choice and negotiating leverage than in the single-family market (Honolulu Star-Advertiser, Sept 7, 2026).
5. HBR's own stated explanation for the split: single-family buyers weigh mainly the purchase price, while condo buyers must also weigh maintenance fees, insurance, and reserve funding as part of true monthly cost — and those carrying costs are shaping what condo buyers will pay (Honolulu Star-Advertiser, Sept 7, 2026).
6. Honest framing: this is one month of data, not a proven trend line — note that June 2026 was actually a hair higher for single-family, so "near-record" is accurate, "record" is not.

## Topic 3 (Ranked #3): Lenders Are Already Moving the 2027 Loan-Limit Line — Before It's Official

### Why it's trending now
This month, Rocket Mortgage and CrossCountry Mortgage both began originating loans at an internal $845,000 conforming loan limit — 1.47% above 2026's official $832,750 baseline — getting ahead of FHFA's official 2027 announcement, which isn't expected until FHFA's Q3 report on November 24, 2026. Because VA loan limits track FHFA's conforming limits, and Honolulu is one of the designated high-cost counties (2026 one-unit VA/conforming limit already $1,249,125), this is an early signal — not yet a fact — about where Hawaii's own high-cost limit is headed, and it's the kind of detail most Hawaii loan officers won't proactively mention.

Sources:
- [Rocket raises conforming loan limit to $845,000 — HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/rocket-conforming-loan-limit/) (Sept 2026)
- [CrossCountry Mortgage lifts internal conforming loan limit to $845K — HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/crosscountry-conforming-loan-limit-845000-fhfa/) (Sept 2026)
- [CrossCountry Mortgage Raises Conforming Loan Limits to $845,000 Ahead of 2027 FHFA Announcement — PR Newswire](http://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/crosscountry-mortgage-raises-conforming-loan-limits-to-845-000-ahead-of-2027-fhfa-announcement-302875681.html)
- [VA Loan Limits for 2026 — NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/blog/mortgages/va-home-loan-limits-disappear-fees-rise/)

### Suggested article
**Title:** "Two Lenders Just Raised the Loan-Limit Ceiling Two Months Early — What It Could Mean for Your VA Loan in Honolulu"

**Want / Problem / Stakes:**
- *Want:* You want to know the real ceiling on how much house you can finance — especially with zero down using your VA entitlement.
- *Problem:* Two national lenders just quietly moved that ceiling higher on their own, months before the government makes anything official, and it won't show up in most local conversations until it's mainstream.
- *Stakes:* A veteran with reduced entitlement house-hunting right at Honolulu's current $1,249,125 limit could get told "no" on a home a lender elsewhere is already willing to finance at a higher number — or could misunderstand this news and assume Hawaii's limit already changed when it hasn't.

### Key facts to include (with citations)
1. Rocket Mortgage announced it is now originating loans at an $845,000 conforming loan limit in 48 states — a 1.47% increase over 2026's official $832,750 baseline — the first major lender to move ahead of FHFA's 2027 announcement (HousingWire, Sept 2026).
2. CrossCountry Mortgage followed with a matching $845,000 internal "Early Bird" limit (HousingWire / PR Newswire, Sept 2026).
3. These are lender-set internal limits, not federal ones. FHFA's official 2027 conforming loan limit isn't expected until its Q3 report, scheduled for November 24, 2026 (HousingWire, Sept 2026).
4. VA loan limits track FHFA's conforming loan limits. In high-cost counties, including Honolulu, the 2026 one-unit limit is already $1,249,125 — well above the $832,750 national baseline (NerdWallet, 2026 VA loan limits).
5. Veterans with full entitlement already have no cap on zero-down VA financing regardless of county limit; the county limit mainly matters for veterans using reduced/partial entitlement (existing VA loan in use) calculating how much they can still borrow with $0 down.
6. Honest caveat the article must state clearly: $845,000 is a national baseline figure from two lenders, not Hawaii's number, and not yet FHFA's number — this is a "here's what to watch for" story, not a "the limit changed" story. Overstating this would mislead readers.
