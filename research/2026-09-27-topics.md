## Topic 1 (rank 1): The property tax exemption most new Oahu homeowners forget to file — deadline is September 30, three days from today

### Why it's trending now
Honolulu's homeowner property tax exemption does not transfer automatically when you buy a home — every owner has to file a new claim, and the deadline to file for the upcoming tax year (beginning July 1, 2027) is **September 30, 2026**. ([Hawaii Life, "Deadline to File a Home Exemption is September 30"](https://www.hawaiilife.com/blog/deadline-to-file-a-home-exemption-is-september-30/); [Locations Hawaii](https://www.locationshawaii.com/news/announcements/file-your-homeowner-exemption-by-september-30/)) That deadline lands this week, which makes it acutely time-sensitive for anyone who closed on an Oahu home in the past year and hasn't filed, or who moved and assumed their old exemption carried over. Layered on top of the routine annual deadline is a live policy story: the Honolulu City Council is currently considering Bill 45 and Bill 46 (2026), which would raise the exemption further — to $160,000 for owners under 65 and $200,000 for owners 65 and older — on top of an already-passed increase (from $120,000/$160,000 to $140,000/$180,000) taking effect July 1, 2027. Bill 46 passed first reading on July 8, 2026, and the Budget Committee discussed both bills on July 14, 2026. ([Grassroot Institute of Hawaii, "Honolulu councilmembers recognize need to increase property tax break"](https://www.grassrootinstitute.org/2026/07/honolulu-committee-endorses-property-tax-break-for-homeowners/)) No existing RealityCents article covers property tax exemptions at all — this is genuinely uncovered ground with a hard deadline attached.

### Suggested article
**Title:** "The Tax Break Most New Oahu Homeowners Forget to Claim — And the September 30 Deadline Is This Week"

**Angle (Want / Problem / Stakes):**
- **Want:** You want your property tax bill to reflect the fact that you actually live in the home you just bought — not the full, unexempted rate.
- **Problem:** The exemption that lowers that bill isn't automatic. It doesn't carry over from your old address, it doesn't get applied just because escrow closed, and the window to file for the coming tax year shuts on September 30 — which is three days from today.
- **Stakes:** Miss the deadline and you pay the city's full, non-exempted property tax rate on your Honolulu home for an entire fiscal year — a real, avoidable cost on top of everything else that's already expensive about owning here. And the exemption itself is about to get more valuable: the Council is actively weighing a further increase, so getting on file now matters for this cycle and the next one.

Honest nuance to preserve: the article should be explicit that the *already-passed* increase (to $140,000/$180,000) doesn't take effect until July 1, 2027, and the *further* increase in Bills 45/46 (to $160,000/$200,000) is still moving through Council as of this writing, not yet law — don't present either as settled beyond what the record shows, and note that Council votes and effective dates can still change.

### Key facts to include (with citations)
1. Filing deadline for the exemption is September 30, 2026; claims filed by that date apply to the tax year beginning July 1, 2027. ([Hawaii Life](https://www.hawaiilife.com/blog/deadline-to-file-a-home-exemption-is-september-30/))
2. The homeowner exemption does not transfer automatically to a new property — recent buyers and anyone who moved must file a new claim. ([Locations Hawaii](https://www.locationshawaii.com/news/announcements/file-your-homeowner-exemption-by-september-30/))
3. Current exemption amounts (through the 2026–2027 tax year): $120,000 for owners under 65 (and for owners whose birthdate isn't on file), $160,000 for owners 65 and older. ([Locations Hawaii](https://www.locationshawaii.com/news/announcements/file-your-homeowner-exemption-by-september-30/))
4. An already-approved increase raises those amounts to $140,000 (under 65) and $180,000 (65+), effective July 1, 2027. ([Locations Hawaii](https://www.locationshawaii.com/news/announcements/file-your-homeowner-exemption-by-september-30/))
5. Bills 45 and 46 (2026), currently moving through the Honolulu City Council, would raise the exemption further to $160,000 (under 65) and $200,000 (65+); Bill 46 passed first reading July 8, 2026, with Budget Committee discussion on July 14, 2026. ([Grassroot Institute of Hawaii](https://www.grassrootinstitute.org/2026/07/honolulu-committee-endorses-property-tax-break-for-homeowners/))
6. City Budget and Fiscal Services Director Andy Kawano told the Committee a $20,000 increase in the exemption amount is worth roughly $70/year in tax relief at current tax rates — a useful, honest scale check against expecting a dramatically lower bill from the increase alone. ([Grassroot Institute of Hawaii](https://www.grassrootinstitute.org/2026/07/honolulu-committee-endorses-property-tax-break-for-homeowners/))

## Topic 2 (rank 2): Rates crossed 7% for a third straight week — and this time buyers are visibly pulling back, not just paying more

### Why it's trending now
This is a direct continuation of the story the site's last two weekly memos and the 9/20 Fed article have been tracking — flagged here specifically because there is a new, different kind of data point this week, not another rate print for its own sake. Freddie Mac's PMMS put the 30-year fixed at **7.03% for the week ending September 24, 2026**, the third consecutive weekly increase (6.71% → 6.95% → 7.03%) and the highest PMMS reading since May 2024. ([Freddie Mac PMMS](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms)) The Mortgage Bankers Association's own weekly benchmark ran slightly hotter, at 7.12% for the week ending September 18 — also a May-2024 high. ([MBA, "Mortgage Applications Decrease in Latest MBA Weekly Survey," 9/23/26](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/newsroom/news/2026/09/23/mortgage-applications-decrease-in-latest-mba-weekly-survey)) What's new this week is the demand-side reaction in that same MBA release: total mortgage applications fell 1.5% week-over-week, the seasonally adjusted Purchase Index fell 1%, and the Refinance Index fell 3% and is now down 62% year-over-year — the slowest pace of refinancing since February 2025. ([MBA, 9/23/26](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/newsroom/news/2026/09/23/mortgage-applications-decrease-in-latest-mba-weekly-survey)) That's a genuinely different story than "rates went up again": it's evidence that, nationally, buyers and refinancers are starting to sit on their hands — a contrast worth drawing against Oahu's own August data (already covered in the site's 9/13 memo) showing single-family homes still selling in a median 17 days at 100% of asking price, i.e., local demand hasn't cooled the way the national numbers suggest it might.

### Suggested article
**Title:** "Rates Just Crossed 7% for the Third Week Running — And Buyers Nationally Are Starting to Back Off. Oahu Isn't."

**Angle (Want / Problem / Stakes):**
- **Want:** You want to know whether the run-up in rates is actually changing your competition for a home, not just your monthly payment.
- **Problem:** Nationally, the data says buyers are pulling back — mortgage applications and especially refinances just dropped to levels not seen in over a year. But Oahu's own August numbers showed homes still selling in 17 days at full asking price, which means the national "buyers are backing off" story may not be true where you're actually shopping.
- **Stakes:** Assuming national headlines about a cooling market apply locally could lead you to underbid, wait, or lose a house you could have gotten — while assuming Oahu is immune to higher rates could mean overextending on a payment you haven't stress-tested against a 7%-plus environment. The honest read is that these are two different markets moving on two different timelines right now, and a buyer needs to know which one they're actually in.

Honest nuance to preserve: don't claim the national pullback proves anything specific about Oahu — the site should be explicit that Hawaii's inventory constraints and military-driven demand have historically decoupled it from mainland demand swings, and that this is one data point, not a trend confirmed over multiple months.

### Key facts to include (with citations)
1. Freddie Mac PMMS 30-year fixed: 7.03% for the week ending Sept 24, 2026, up from 6.95% (Sept 17) and 6.71% (Sept 3) — three straight weekly increases. ([Freddie Mac PMMS](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms))
2. 15-year fixed: 6.42% (Sept 24), up from 6.26% the prior week. ([Freddie Mac PMMS](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms))
3. MBA's own survey rate hit 7.12% for the week ending Sept 18, 2026 — the highest level since May 2024. ([MBA, 9/23/26](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/newsroom/news/2026/09/23/mortgage-applications-decrease-in-latest-mba-weekly-survey))
4. Total mortgage applications fell 1.5% week-over-week; the seasonally adjusted Purchase Index fell 1%; the unadjusted Purchase Index was 11% lower than the same week a year ago. ([MBA, 9/23/26](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/newsroom/news/2026/09/23/mortgage-applications-decrease-in-latest-mba-weekly-survey))
5. The Refinance Index fell 3% week-over-week and 62% year-over-year, with the refinance share of activity slipping to 39.3% of total applications — the slowest refinancing pace since February 2025. ([MBA, 9/23/26](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/newsroom/news/2026/09/23/mortgage-applications-decrease-in-latest-mba-weekly-survey))
6. For local contrast, Oahu single-family homes sold in a median 17 days at 100% of original list price in August 2026 (per the site's own prior research citing HBR/Sotheby's data) — the article should present this as context from a different month's data set, not as evidence that August behavior is still holding in late September.

## Topic 3 (rank 3): The Fannie/Freddie condo full-review rule has been live for two months — and a new industry survey shows it's already hurting sales

### Why it's trending now
The site's May 17, 2026 article on Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac's condo project-review overhaul was written prospectively, ahead of the rule taking effect. The rule — full retirement of Fannie's Limited Review and Freddie's Streamlined Review pathways, requiring a full financial and structural review for most condo purchases — actually took effect August 3, 2026 and has now been in force for roughly two months. ([CNBC, "Condo buyers face new mortgage rules under Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac," 8/1/26](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/01/condo-buyers-mortgage-rules-fannie-mae-freddie-mac.html)) What's new since the May article is real-world outcome data: the Foundation for Community Association Research surveyed more than 700 condo/co-op board members, managers, and business partners and found 42% are unsure whether their own association is currently eligible for Fannie/Freddie financing, 40% believe their community has characteristics (deferred maintenance, insufficient reserves, pending litigation) that could trigger ineligibility, and — among associations already deemed ineligible — 64% reported the denial had negatively affected home sales or property values in their community. ([Community Associations Institute Advocacy Blog](https://advocacy.caionline.org/new-guidance-helps-navigate-fannie-mae-and-freddie-mac-lending-eligibility/)) That turns this from "a rule that's coming" into "a rule that's actively affecting sales right now" — a legitimate, data-backed sequel to the May piece, and directly relevant to a Hawaii audience where condos are a large share of transactions and already the subject of the site's own insurance-crisis coverage.

### Suggested article
**Title:** "The New Condo Full-Review Rule Has Been Live Two Months — Here's What the Data Now Shows About Who's Getting Denied"

**Angle (Want / Problem / Stakes):**
- **Want:** You want to know, before you write an offer on a Hawaii condo, whether the building itself can actually get financed under today's rules — not the rules as they stood when you last looked.
- **Problem:** The full-review requirement isn't hypothetical anymore. It's been live since August, and a new national survey of condo boards shows a meaningful share don't know their own eligibility status, and that ineligibility is already showing up as depressed sales and property values in real buildings.
- **Stakes:** Falling in love with a specific unit before confirming the building's project-review status can cost you weeks in escrow, a denied loan, or — if enough buyers in that building face the same issue — real, measurable downward pressure on what the unit is worth. This is a question to ask before you offer, not after.

Honest nuance to preserve: the CAI survey is a snap self-reported survey of board members and managers, not a random sample of all U.S. condo associations — the article should say so plainly rather than present 64% as a precise national ineligibility-impact rate, and should note that a denial from one lender doesn't automatically mean a unit is unfinanceable, since some lenders portfolio loans rather than sell to Fannie/Freddie.

### Key facts to include (with citations)
1. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac fully retired the Limited Review and Streamlined Review pathways, requiring a full project review for most condo purchases, effective August 3, 2026. ([CNBC, 8/1/26](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/01/condo-buyers-mortgage-rules-fannie-mae-freddie-mac.html))
2. Before the change, roughly 40% of condo mortgage reviews used one of the now-retired limited/streamlined pathways. ([CNBC, 8/1/26](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/01/condo-buyers-mortgage-rules-fannie-mae-freddie-mac.html))
3. The Foundation for Community Association Research's snap survey of 700+ board members, managers, and business partners found 42% are unsure whether their association is Fannie/Freddie-eligible. ([CAI Advocacy Blog](https://advocacy.caionline.org/new-guidance-helps-navigate-fannie-mae-and-freddie-mac-lending-eligibility/))
4. 40% of survey respondents said their community may have characteristics — deferred maintenance, insufficient reserves, pending litigation — that could trigger ineligibility. ([CAI Advocacy Blog](https://advocacy.caionline.org/new-guidance-helps-navigate-fannie-mae-and-freddie-mac-lending-eligibility/))
5. Among associations already deemed ineligible, 64% of respondents said the denial had negatively affected home sales or property values in their community. ([CAI Advocacy Blog](https://advocacy.caionline.org/new-guidance-helps-navigate-fannie-mae-and-freddie-mac-lending-eligibility/))
6. A denial under one investor's guidelines doesn't automatically make a unit unfinanceable — some lenders hold VA and other loans in portfolio rather than selling to Fannie or Freddie, which is a distinction the site's existing `va-condo-approval-vs-warrantability-hawaii` article already makes for VA buyers specifically and should be linked, not re-explained from scratch. (Cross-reference to site's own article, not an external citation.)
