## Topic 1 (rank 1): The 2027 VA Disability/COLA Raise Is Official — and It Changes What Some Veterans Can Qualify For

### Why it's trending now
The Social Security Administration announced a 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment for 2027 on October 14, 2026 — four days before this memo — and VA disability compensation is set to rise by the same percentage, the largest COLA in three years (up from 2.8% in 2026 and 2.5% in 2025). ([Newsweek, "2027 COLA Increase: What It Means for VA Disability Benefits," Oct 2026](https://www.newsweek.com/2027-cola-increase-what-means-va-disability-benefits-12571215); [CCK Law, "2027 VA Disability Pay Rates and Cost-of-Living Adjustment"](https://cck-law.com/blog/2027-va-disability-pay-rates-and-cost-of-living-adjustment/)) The increase is scheduled to take effect December 1, 2026, but as of October 15 the Veterans' Compensation Cost-of-Living Adjustment Act of 2026 — the bill that actually authorizes the VA to pay it — had not yet been enacted by Congress, and the VA has not published official 2027 payment tables. Dollar figures already circulating online (one source cites $4,080.37/month for a 100% rating with no dependents) are estimates, not official numbers. ([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/2027-cola-increase-what-means-va-disability-benefits-12571215); [beinsure.com, "2027 VA Disability Rates Increase"](https://beinsure.com/news/2027-va-disability-rates-increase/))

This is squarely relevant to RealityCents' core audience, and it's genuinely new ground: no KB article addresses how a COLA increase interacts with mortgage qualifying, and no prior weekly-research memo has covered it — the Oct 11 memo explicitly flagged it as "premature" to publish projected percentages and set it aside for "once the official figure is out." That's now.

### Suggested article
**Title:** "Your VA Disability Pay Is Going Up 3.6% in December — Here's What That Does (and Doesn't Do) for Your Mortgage"

**Angle (Want/Problem/Stakes):**
- *Want:* You want to know whether this raise actually moves the needle on what house you can afford, and when.
- *Problem:* The 3.6% increase is announced but not yet law, the real December dollar figures aren't published, and VA disability income is counted differently in mortgage underwriting than ordinary income — most "COLA is coming" coverage doesn't touch any of that.
- *Stakes:* Shop for a house today using an estimated post-raise number you found online, and a lender who counts your current (lower, pre-December) disability payment could qualify you for less than you expected — or you could misjudge your exemption status on the VA funding fee and build a budget around savings that don't apply to you.

### Key facts for the article (with citations)
1. SSA announced a 3.6% COLA for 2027 on October 14, 2026; VA disability compensation is set to increase by the same percentage, pending congressional enactment of the Veterans' Compensation COLA Act of 2026. ([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/2027-cola-increase-what-means-va-disability-benefits-12571215))
2. 3.6% is the largest VA COLA since 2025, up from 2.8% in 2026 and 2.5% in 2025. ([CCK Law](https://cck-law.com/blog/2027-va-disability-pay-rates-and-cost-of-living-adjustment/))
3. The increase is scheduled for December 1, 2026, with first adjusted payments expected around December 31, 2026 — but as of mid-October the authorizing legislation had not yet passed, and the VA's official 2027 rate tables are not yet published; any specific dollar figures in circulation should be labeled estimates. ([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/2027-cola-increase-what-means-va-disability-benefits-12571215))
4. The adjustment affects more than 6 million veterans currently receiving VA disability compensation nationwide. ([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/2027-cola-increase-what-means-va-disability-benefits-12571215))
5. Mortgage-relevant mechanics worth restating from RealityCents' own existing coverage, tied back to this news: VA disability compensation is non-taxable and lenders can typically "gross up" that income for qualifying purposes, and veterans receiving VA disability compensation are exempt from the VA funding fee regardless of down payment size. (Internal: `/knowledge-base/va-funding-fee-hawaii`)
6. Because the new payment amount doesn't land until a December 2026 check, a pre-approval run in October or November will necessarily use the current, lower monthly figure — borrowers whose qualifying math is close to the line should ask their lender directly how (and when) the increase will be reflected, rather than assume it is already built in.

**Honest nuance to include:** be explicit that the raise is not law yet (Congress still has to pass the COLA Act, which is routine but not guaranteed on a specific date), that the VA hasn't published real dollar tables, and that a COLA increase does not change funding-fee exemption rules or entitlement — it only changes the income figure a lender can use once it's actually being paid.

## Topic 2 (rank 2): The HOA Number on Your Pre-Approval Isn't the HOA Number You'll Close With

### Why it's trending now
This builds on, rather than repeats, RealityCents' own `fannie-mae-condo-guidelines-2026-hawaii` article, which covers Fannie Mae's project-level reserve requirement rising from 10% to 15% of an association's budgeted income on January 4, 2027, and the building-level consequence (a building that doesn't meet the new threshold can lose conventional financing eligibility entirely). What that article doesn't cover — and what's sharpening into its own story as the January 4 deadline approaches — is the individual-buyer consequence: associations raising dues now to get ahead of the reserve deadline, which can push a specific buyer's debt-to-income ratio past approval even in a building that stays fully warrantable. Two supporting data points, both newer than the condo-guidelines article's own research: UHERO put Oahu's median condo HOA fee at roughly $882/month as of February 2026 ([Hawaii Life, citing UHERO](https://www.hawaiilife.com/blog/navigating-hawaiis-rising-homeowners-insurance-rates-selling-buying-property/)), and the March–April 2026 "Kona Low" storms are still working through insurers' 2026–27 renewal pricing on top of that. ([Hawaii Tribune-Herald](https://www.hawaiitribune-herald.com/?p=341062))

**Flag for Jay's team:** confirm this angle (individual DTI math, dues increases timed to the Jan 4, 2027 reserve deadline) reads as distinct from the existing condo-guidelines article before publishing — the underlying rule is the same, but the audience question is different ("will *my* loan still work" vs. "will *this building* still qualify").

### Suggested article
**Title:** "Your Condo Building Passed Fannie Mae's Reserve Rule. Your Own DTI Might Not Survive the Dues Increase That Paid for It."

**Angle (Want/Problem/Stakes):**
- *Want:* You want the condo you're under contract on to actually close at the payment you were pre-approved for.
- *Problem:* Associations racing to meet Fannie Mae's January 4, 2027 reserve requirement are raising dues now — and a dues increase that shows up on a new HOA statement mid-escrow can push your debt-to-income ratio past approval even though the building itself remains fully financeable.
- *Stakes:* Find out after your inspection period closes and your rate lock is running, and you can be stuck re-qualifying, renegotiating, or losing your earnest money — on a building that, on paper, did everything right.

### Key facts for the article (with citations)
1. Fannie Mae's minimum reserve-allocation requirement for condo associations rises from 10% to 15% of annual budgeted assessment income starting January 4, 2027 — already covered on RealityCents, but worth restating as the direct cause of dues increases buyers are now seeing. (Internal: `/knowledge-base/fannie-mae-condo-guidelines-2026-hawaii`)
2. Oahu's median condo HOA fee was roughly $882/month as of February 2026 per UHERO data, with older buildings often running higher because of special assessments layered on top. ([Hawaii Life](https://www.hawaiilife.com/blog/navigating-hawaiis-rising-homeowners-insurance-rates-selling-buying-property/))
3. Statewide property insurance premiums rose roughly 13.4% in 2024, and the March–April 2026 Kona Low storms added further claims pressure still feeding into 2026–27 renewal pricing — a second, independent driver of dues increases layered on top of the reserve-rule deadline. ([Hawaii Tribune-Herald](https://www.hawaiitribune-herald.com/?p=341062))
4. Conventional lenders generally calculate DTI using the current, full monthly HOA dues figure as a housing expense — meaning a dues increase that posts after pre-approval but before closing can require a full re-run of the borrower's ratios, not just a note on the file.
5. An association using Hawaii's "cash flow plan" reserve method (permitted under HRS §514B-148(a)(8)) that hasn't also raised its budget allocation to 15% risks losing conventional financing eligibility altogether — in that scenario, the dues increase is the association's way of avoiding that outcome, not an unrelated cost spike. (Internal: `/knowledge-base/fannie-mae-condo-guidelines-2026-hawaii`)
6. Buyers should ask for the association's most recent board minutes and reserve study before writing an offer, specifically to see whether a dues increase tied to the January 2027 deadline has already been approved but not yet billed.

**Honest nuance to include:** concede plainly that this is a second-order effect of a rule RealityCents has already explained — the value of this piece is the buyer-level qualifying-math angle, not a new regulatory development. Don't overstate how common a mid-escrow DTI failure actually is; frame it as a real but avoidable risk that a buyer can screen for upfront.

## Topic 3 (rank 3): Two More Major Lenders Just Jumped Into the 2027 Loan-Limit Race, and the Top Number Just Climbed to $850,000

### Why it's trending now
This is an update, not a new story: RealityCents' own research flagged lenders front-running FHFA's 2027 conforming loan limit back on `2026-09-13`, when only Rocket Mortgage and CrossCountry Mortgage had set an early internal limit of $845,000. Since then, two more major lenders have joined at higher figures — UWM at $847,440 and Pennymac at $850,000, the highest of the group, effective immediately across Pennymac's retail, wholesale, and correspondent channels. ([National Mortgage Professional, "Three Competing 2027 Conforming Loan Limits — And None Is Official"](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/three-competing-2027-conforming-loan-limits-and-none-official); [NMP — Pennymac](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/pennymac-raises-conforming-loan-limit-above-early-industry-benchmark); [NMP — UWM](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/uwm-splits-difference-early-2027-loan-limit-race)) FHFA's own number is still not out and isn't expected until its Q3 report, now reported as scheduled for November 24, 2026. The practical problem for a borrower hasn't changed since September — it's just gotten more confusing, with four lenders now quoting three different numbers instead of two lenders quoting one.

This is included as a lower-ranked, explicitly-labeled update rather than a full new topic, consistent with how this site's own research handled the October 1 rate jump as an escalation of an ongoing rate story rather than a fresh one.

### Suggested article
**Title:** "Update: A Fourth Lender Just Raised Its Own 2027 Loan Limit to $850,000 — Still Not FHFA's Number"
*(Best run as a short update/sidebar on the existing loan-limit coverage rather than a full standalone article, given how recently this site covered the underlying story.)*

**Angle (Want/Problem/Stakes):**
- *Want:* You want a straight answer on whether your loan amount is "conforming" or "jumbo" before you lock a rate.
- *Problem:* Four different lenders are now quoting three different early 2027 numbers ($845,000, $847,440, $850,000), and the one number that actually matters — FHFA's official figure — still isn't out, with a new expected date of November 24, 2026.
- *Stakes:* Shop rate quotes across lenders without knowing which early number each one is using, and you can end up comparing a jumbo quote at one lender against a conventional quote at another for what should be the same loan.

### Key facts for the article (with citations)
1. Rocket Mortgage and CrossCountry Mortgage set an early 2027 one-unit limit of $845,000 in September 2026; UWM has since set $847,440, and Pennymac set the highest figure yet, $850,000, effective across all of its origination channels. ([NMP](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/three-competing-2027-conforming-loan-limits-and-none-official); [NMP — UWM](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/uwm-splits-difference-early-2027-loan-limit-race); [NMP — Pennymac](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/pennymac-raises-conforming-loan-limit-above-early-industry-benchmark))
2. The official 2026 one-unit baseline remains $832,750; none of the four lenders' figures is an FHFA number. ([NMP](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/three-competing-2027-conforming-loan-limits-and-none-official))
3. FHFA's 2027 announcement is expected with its Q3 House Price Index report, now reported as scheduled for November 24, 2026. ([NMP](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/three-competing-2027-conforming-loan-limits-and-none-official))
4. Loans originated above the current official 2026 limit cannot be sold to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac until the 2027 limit is actually in effect, regardless of which early number a lender advertises. ([NMP](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/three-competing-2027-conforming-loan-limits-and-none-official))
5. Hawaii's high-cost county ceilings move with the national baseline (set as a percentage multiple of it), so the eventual FHFA number — whichever of the competing figures it lands closest to — will also move Oahu's, Maui's, Kauai's, and Hawaii County's 2027 ceilings; RealityCents' own VA loan limits page has the current 2026 figures for reference. (Internal: `/knowledge-base/va-loan-limits-hawaii-2026`)

**Honest nuance to include:** state clearly this is the same unresolved story from five weeks ago, now with two more data points — not a new development in its own right. Recommend readers ask their own lender which number is actually underwriting their file, rather than assume the highest-quoted figure applies.
