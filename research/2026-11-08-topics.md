## Topic 1 (rank 1): Oahu's October Numbers Just Landed — Condo Sales Fell ~23%, But Prices Barely Moved

### Why it's trending now
Fresh October 2026 Oahu market data posted in the last 24–48 hours (List Sotheby's report dated 2026-11-07; corroborated independently by Locations Hawaii's market report). Last week's memo flagged this exact gap — "October's HBR data isn't released until roughly November 7, so there's no newer Oahu print to report yet" — so this is the follow-through on a story this research pipeline was already watching for. September's data (reported 2026-10-07) showed the same softening starting a month earlier, so this is a two-month pattern, not a one-off.

- List Sotheby's (2026-11-07): single-family sales down 8.4% YoY to 239 transactions; median price down 1.1% YoY to $1,150,000; condo sales down 23.5% YoY to 339 transactions; condo median down 6.7% YoY to $499,000; single-family days-on-market fell to a median of 15 days vs. 26 a year ago. — [listsothebysrealty.com/2026/11/07/oahu-real-estate-market-report-october-2026](https://listsothebysrealty.com/2026/11/07/oahu-real-estate-market-report-october-2026)
- Locations Hawaii's independent October read is close but not identical: single-family flat (0%) at $1,170,000; condo median down ~6% to $499,900; sales down ~9% (single-family) and ~23% (condo). — [locationshawaii.com/learn/market-reports/oahu-real-estate-report](https://www.locationshawaii.com/learn/market-reports/oahu-real-estate-report/)
- Star-Advertiser (2026-10-07), on September: previously-owned single-family median fell 3.9% YoY to $1,110,000. — [staradvertiser.com/2026/10/07/hawaii-news/oahu-single-family-home-price-weakened-in-september](https://www.staradvertiser.com/2026/10/07/hawaii-news/oahu-single-family-home-price-weakened-in-september/)

**Honest caveat:** the two private October reports disagree by a point or two on every figure — normal for competing MLS cuts ahead of the Honolulu Board of REALTORS®' own release. Say "roughly" on the specific percentages, link out to both, and swap in HBR's own number once it posts at hicentral.com rather than presenting one source's cut as the official figure.

### Suggested article
**Title:** "Oahu Condo Sales Just Fell 23%. Here's Why Your Dream Unit Still Isn't Any Cheaper."

**Want/Problem/Stakes opener:**
- **Want:** You want the market to finally turn in your favor after two years of losing bidding wars.
- **Problem:** Condo sales on Oahu just dropped roughly 23% year-over-year and single-family listings are selling in barely two weeks — the "crash" isn't showing up in the price tag.
- **Stakes:** Wait for a discount that may not come, and you could spend another year renting while the next buyer who moves fast gets the unit you wanted — at roughly the same price you're waiting to avoid.

**Angle:** Explain why volume can fall sharply while price barely moves (sellers who don't need to sell are pulling listings rather than cutting price; the buyers left in the market are serious and well-qualified, which is why days-on-market actually dropped). Give a concrete decision framework: in a low-volume, low-DOM market, buyer leverage shows up in negotiating *terms* — concessions, inspection contingencies, rate buydowns — not in a lower list price. Link to the site's existing seller-concessions and rate-buydown articles as the next, practical step.

### Key facts for the article (with citations)
1. October 2026 Oahu condo sales fell roughly 23% year-over-year even as the condo median price fell a more modest ~6–7% — a mismatch worth explaining plainly. (List Sotheby's, Locations Hawaii, Nov 2026)
2. Single-family days-on-market dropped to a median of 15 days in October 2026, down from 26 days a year earlier — homes that do sell are moving faster, not slower. (List Sotheby's, 2026-11-07)
3. September 2026 single-family median was down 3.9% YoY to $1,110,000, showing the softening isn't a one-month fluke. (Star-Advertiser, 2026-10-07)
4. The two independent October reads disagree slightly on exact percentages — a reminder that "the market" is reported differently depending on whose MLS cut you're reading; wait for HBR's own release before quoting a number as definitive.
5. This reads as a volume correction, not (yet) a price correction — a distinction that matters directly to anyone deciding whether to wait for a "deal."

## Topic 2 (rank 2): A December 11 Funding Deadline Is Coming — Here's What a Repeat Shutdown Would Actually Do to Your Closing (Not Your Rate)

### Why it's trending now
This is deliberately framed away from the rate/Fed story this site has already run four times this fall. The angle here is narrower and unused: Congress's stopgap bill, signed ahead of the midterms, only funds the government through **December 11, 2026**, and the Senate still hasn't passed full-year FY2027 appropriations. The prior lapse — a documented 43-day shutdown — produced real, dated delays to FHA insurance endorsements and VA loan processing, not speculation. For anyone with a closing scheduled from early December into January, that's a live, concrete operational risk that has nothing to do with where the 30-year rate goes.

- The stopgap funding bill, signed ahead of the midterms, extends government funding only through December 11, 2026; the Senate has not yet passed full-year FY2027 spending bills. — [pulse.bot: "Historic Government Shutdown Finally Ends, but FY27 Funding Deadline Looms"](https://www.pulse.bot/us-economy/news/historic-government-shutdown-finally-ends-but-fy27-funding-deadline-looms-c4eb0b42-55b2-47d9-ad74-0b399657b5bc/)
- The prior (2025) shutdown ran 43 days and produced confirmed delays to FHA insurance endorsements and VA loan processing — documented history, not a forecast. — [san.com/cc/federal-housing-programs-back-online-after-government-shutdown-ends](https://san.com/cc/federal-housing-programs-back-online-after-government-shutdown-ends/)
- During that lapse, USDA Rural Development's guaranteed housing loan program was the hardest-hit federal mortgage program, with new commitments effectively frozen — a useful comparison point for how unevenly a shutdown can hit different loan types (VA and conventional kept moving with delays; USDA stopped outright). — [National Mortgage News coverage referenced in this research session; verify against USDA Rural Development's own program notices before publishing a specific freeze date]

**Honest caveat:** we could not confirm, in this research session, whether an earlier "partial shutdown" some sources dated to January 30, 2026 ever meaningfully disrupted VA/FHA processing — that episode was thinly and inconsistently sourced. The article should rely only on the two confirmed, dated facts above (the Dec 11 deadline and the documented 2025 shutdown precedent), not on that uncertain middle episode.

### Suggested article
**Title:** "Washington Has Until December 11 to Avoid Another Shutdown. Here's What That Actually Means for Your Closing Date — Not Your Rate."

**Want/Problem/Stakes opener:**
- **Want:** You want your closing to happen on schedule, with no surprises in the paperwork.
- **Problem:** Government funding runs out December 11, 2026, and the last time that happened, FHA insurance endorsements and VA loan processing both backed up for weeks — while USDA's rural loan program froze outright.
- **Stakes:** A buyer who assumes "the government shutting down" is a Washington story, not a homebuying one, can get blindsided by a funding-fee submission or an FHA endorsement that simply can't move until Congress acts — right when they thought they were just waiting on an appraisal.

**Angle:** This is a practical, process-focused piece, not a rate-prediction piece — be explicit about that distinction up front, since this site has already covered the Fed/rate angle on the October 28 decision at length. Give readers a plain checklist: ask your lender now whether your loan type (VA, FHA, conventional, USDA) relies on any federal step that pauses during a lapse; build schedule buffer into any closing date that falls on or after December 11; and know that a VA or conventional closing is far more insulated than a USDA one.

### Key facts for the article (with citations)
1. Government funding is secured only through December 11, 2026; full-year FY2027 appropriations have not passed the Senate as of this writing.
2. The 2025 shutdown lasted 43 days and produced confirmed delays to FHA insurance endorsements and VA loan processing.
3. USDA's guaranteed rural housing loan program was the hardest-hit mortgage program in the prior lapse, with new loan commitments effectively frozen — a materially different risk profile than VA or conventional financing. **Verify the exact freeze dates and current USDA guidance directly with USDA Rural Development's Hawaii office before publishing specifics**, since this was not independently confirmed to a primary source in this research session.
4. VA and FHA loans generally kept moving during the prior lapse with added lag, rather than stopping outright — worth stating plainly so the piece doesn't overstate the risk for the audience's two core loan types.
5. A rate lock has its own expiration date, and a federal processing delay can eat into that window independently — two separate risks that can compound around the same closing date, which is the reason this is worth a standalone piece rather than a footnote on a rate article.

## Topic 3 (rank 3, flagged sourcing caveats): Honolulu's Homeowner Property Tax Exemption Is Going Up for the First Time in Years — But the Numbers in Circulation Don't Agree

### Why it's trending now
This is new ground for the site — no prior weekly memo and no existing article touches Honolulu's property tax home exemption. The Honolulu City Council passed Bills 49 and 50 in September 2025, raising the base and kupuna (65+) homeowner exemptions effective with the tax year beginning **July 1, 2027** — directly relevant to anyone doing total-cost-of-ownership math on an Oahu purchase today, since 2027 is inside a normal closing-to-first-property-tax-bill horizon. The story has a live, moving piece too: a second round of 2026-session bills (Bill 45 and Bill 46) addressing the same exemptions was reported out of committee in July 2026, with final-passage status unconfirmed as of this research.

- Two 2025 measures, Bill 49 and Bill 50, passed and take effect for the tax year beginning July 1, 2027: Bill 49 raises the kupuna exemption from $160,000 to $180,000; Bill 50 raises the base homeowner exemption from $120,000 to $140,000. — [locationshawaii.com/news/announcements/oahu-homeowners-to-see-property-tax-relief-starting-in-2027](https://www.locationshawaii.com/news/announcements/oahu-homeowners-to-see-property-tax-relief-starting-in-2027)
- A separate, later-dated report (KITV) describes substantially larger final figures for the same two bills — Bill 50 to $250,000 and Bill 49 to $300,000 — which appear to reflect an earlier draft version rather than what was ultimately enacted. — [kitv.com/news/honolulu-property-tax-exemptions-could-ease-living-costs-for-kupuna](https://www.kitv.com/news/honolulu-property-tax-exemptions-could-ease-living-costs-for-kupuna/article_07c1f4a0-7e90-47df-acee-2be9d574e345.html)
- A 2026-session follow-on, Bill 45 (base exemption) and Bill 46 (kupuna exemption), both sponsored by Council Chair Tommy Waters, were reported out of the Budget Committee for a public hearing on July 14, 2026 — but we could not confirm final passage, a vote date, or final dollar amounts for either bill in this research session.

**Honest caveat — this is the one to lead with in any editorial conversation:** the dollar figures for this story conflict meaningfully across sources ($140,000/$180,000 vs. $250,000/$300,000 for the 2025 bills), and the 2026 follow-on bills' status is unconfirmed. **Do not publish specific exemption dollar amounts until they're verified directly against the enacted ordinance text at hnldoc.ehawaii.gov or the Honolulu Real Property Assessment Division (realproperty.honolulu.gov).** The safe, confirmed claim is narrower than the headline: Honolulu's council has raised (2025) and is actively considering raising further (2026) the homeowner exemption, effective 2027, after a long period without a base-exemption increase — that direction is solid; the specific numbers are not yet safe to print.

### Suggested article
**Title:** "Honolulu's Homeowner Tax Exemption Is Going Up in 2027. Here's What We Can Confirm — and What We Can't Yet."

**Want/Problem/Stakes opener:**
- **Want:** You want an honest number for what your actual property tax bill will look like a year or two after you close on an Oahu home.
- **Problem:** The City Council has passed one increase to the homeowner exemption (effective 2027) and is actively considering a second one in 2026 — but the dollar figures being reported publicly don't agree with each other, and the newer bills haven't finished moving through Council.
- **Stakes:** Budgeting around the wrong exemption number — in either direction — throws off a real affordability calculation; this is exactly the kind of detail that should be verified against the Council's own enacted text before a buyer leans on it, not read off a headline.

**Angle:** This should be framed candidly as "here's the confirmed direction, here's what's still moving, verify before you rely on a number" — consistent with the site's own house style of conceding nuance rather than presenting a tidy number. Cross-reference the site's affordability pieces (`income-needed-buy-home-hawaii-2026`, `down-payment-myth-hawaii-pmi-vs-appreciation`) as where a confirmed exemption figure would eventually plug in.

### Key facts for the article (with citations)
1. Honolulu City Council Bills 49 and 50 (2025) raise the homeowner exemptions for the tax year beginning July 1, 2027 — the direction and effective date are confirmed; the exact dollar figures are not (two sources disagree substantially).
2. A homeowner exemption reduces the assessed value subject to property tax before the rate is applied — relevant background for readers unfamiliar with how Honolulu's property tax actually works, independent of the exact dollar figures in dispute.
3. A second, 2026-session pair of bills (45 and 46), also addressing the homeowner and kupuna exemptions, cleared committee for a public hearing in July 2026; final passage status is unconfirmed as of this research and must be checked before publishing.

This is the kind of detail a real-estate agent partner audience will want sourced precisely (it affects buyer net-cost conversations) — which is exactly why the article should hold off on specific numbers until Jay's team verifies them against the enacted ordinance, not round up from a press clipping.
